Microsoft just published in Science that their AI got hard medical cases right 83% of the time. Generalist doctors, with textbooks and internet access, got 36%.

Everyone will share this as "AI beats doctors." That's not the interesting part.

The interesting part is how it works. It doesn't answer in one shot. It acts like a panel: one agent proposes a diagnosis, another argues against it, another asks whether the next test is worth the cost. Then it orders tests one at a time, the way a real doctor would.

That's the pattern I keep seeing everywhere right now. The big jumps aren't coming from one smarter model. They're coming from models arguing with each other.

The caveats are real. Text-only cases, no physical exams, compared against generalists not specialists, and no live patients yet. A prospective study with Beth Israel in Boston is next.

If that one holds up, the first place this lands isn't replacing doctors. It's the rural clinic with no specialist within 200 miles.
